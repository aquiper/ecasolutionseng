BS 7910 · API 1104 Annex A

Fracture mechanics

Fracture mechanics establishes the flaw size a component can tolerate for its intended life, and how long a flaw that is already there will take to matter. The codes of record are BS 7910 and API 1104 Annex A.

Typical applications

  • Girth weld acceptance criteria for new construction
  • Reassessment of existing welds against PAUT or radiographic indications
  • Fatigue life under flow-induced vibration, pressure cycling or thermal cycling
  • Installation strain — reeling, S-lay, J-lay and cold field bends
  • Alternative acceptance criteria where workmanship standards are unnecessarily restrictive

Where the answer usually comes from

Toughness. An engineering critical assessment is only as good as its fracture toughness input, and code-default values are frequently the reason a sound weld gets condemned. Where it matters, toughness is taken from production weld rings or CTOD testing rather than assumed.

What you receive

Tolerable flaw dimensions by position and orientation, fatigue life against a stated stress spectrum, and the sensitivity of the answer to each input — so you know which number to go and measure if the margin is thin.
