Case study 02 · MIC screening

MIC screening of a 55-day seawater hold.

Operator
Redacted
System
Two rigid flowlines flooded with untreated seawater ahead of commissioning. No biocide, no pigging, no inhibition, no flow. Seabed temperature 4.5 °C, hold period 55 days, against a 3.0 mm corrosion allowance and 23.29 mm wall.
Question
Did the hold period justify emergency biocide treatment, or could the operator proceed to dewatering as planned?
Approach
EC&A MIC Risk Evaluator v1.7 — a bounded-factor screening tool aligned to NACE SP0208-2018, scoring 14 environmental, microbiological and operational parameters. SRB and NRB presence assumed conservatively in the absence of field microbiology. Run alongside a first-principles oxygen corrosion prediction along the full 29.3 km.
Finding
Risk index 49.5/100 — Elevated. Driven by stagnation (22.0 of score) and assumed SRB with 2,700 mg/L sulfate available. Pitting rate 0.487 mm/y. But at 4.5 °C the temperature factor scored 0.05 of 1.0, and 55 days is short: maximum pitting depth 0.073 mm, 0.31% of wall. Oxygen corrosion added 0.108 mm at the inlet, falling below 0.01 mm/y past KP 5.8 km as dissolved O2 was consumed.
Outcome
Combined worst case 0.181 mm — 0.78% of wall, about 6% of the corrosion allowance. Dewater as planned; no biocide. An elevated risk classification did not mean elevated damage. Recommendation carried a hard boundary: past 90 days the arithmetic changes and biocide becomes warranted.
