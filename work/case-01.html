Case study 01 · Corrosion and materials

The corrosion allowance was 3 mm. The answer was 6.

Operator
Redacted
System
Two rigid production flowlines, 10.75 in OD × 23.29 mm wall, Grade 450 seamless to DNV-ST-F101. 29.3 km each, in 1,058–1,605 m of water. Produced fluids carried 3.03 mol% CO2 in the gas phase, 135,000 ppmw chloride, 344 ppmw bicarbonate, no H2S. Sweet corrosion governed.
Question
Was the 3.0 mm corrosion allowance carried in the design sufficient for a 29-year life, and what corrosion inhibitor availability would the operator have to actually hold to make it work?
Approach
ECE v5.9 run year by year across the full production forecast — declining oil from 17,844 to 1,335 StdBOPD with water cut climbing past 96% — at inlet and outlet of both flowlines, four cases per scenario. Uninhibited base case first, then sensitivity on inhibitor availability at 95% and 98%. Section runs at 1,000 increments along the as-built seabed profile located the worst position, which was not the inlet.
Finding
Uninhibited: 32.8 to 61.3 mm cumulative wall loss over 29 years, against a 23.29 mm wall. At 95% CI availability: 4.47–5.89 mm. At 98%: 3.57–4.14 mm. Both exceed 3.0 mm. Both sit inside 6.0 mm.
Outcome
Recommendation: 6.0 mm corrosion allowance, not 3.0 mm. At 98% availability that leaves more than 19 mm of structural wall — 15–18% consumed over full design life. Moving availability from 95% to 98% was worth a 20–26% reduction in wall loss, which reframed inhibitor redundancy from a running cost into a wall-thickness decision. Peak rate found at KP 7.7 km, not at either end, so monitoring placement changed too.
